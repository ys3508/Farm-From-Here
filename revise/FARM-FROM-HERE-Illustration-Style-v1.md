# FARM FROM HERE — Illustration & Visual Aesthetic Guide v1

## Purpose

This document defines the **visual art direction** for all FARM FROM HERE illustrations and generated imagery.

The goal is **a consistent artistic language, not a fixed visual inventory**.

Future images may contain completely different subjects — farms, trees, plants, animals, companion creatures, pets, people, landscapes, objects, maps, seasonal scenes, or imaginative elements — as long as they feel like they were created by the **same illustrator, in the same visual world, using the same artistic materials and visual principles**.

### Important principle

**Lock the art style. Do not lock the subject matter.**

Do NOT treat any particular object, character, composition, landscape, animal, doorway, dog, windmill, or other motif as a required recurring element.

Creative freedom is encouraged.

---

## 1. Core Visual Identity

### Primary style

**Hand-drawn pastoral storybook illustration**

FARM FROM HERE should feel like:

> **A living real world, drawn like a warm countryside storybook.**

The visual world should be:
- warm
- natural
- dreamy but restrained
- peaceful
- hopeful
- tactile
- slightly whimsical
- grounded in the real world

It should feel like a countryside picture book made with colored pencils and soft crayons — not like a generic children's cartoon.

### The central idea

The product is about the **real world**, but presented as a world that feels worth exploring.

Therefore:

**Real-world grounding + storybook imagination**

The scene, subject, and story can change freely. The consistent element is the **way the world is illustrated**.

---

## 2. Illustration Medium & Texture

The defining material language is:

**colored pencil + soft crayon + textured paper**

Images should visibly contain:
- fine colored-pencil strokes
- soft crayon texture
- subtle grain
- layered hand-drawn marks
- slightly imperfect linework
- uneven, organic color application
- visible directional strokes where appropriate
- tactile paper-like surface

The artwork should feel **made by hand**, not digitally rendered to look perfect.

### Avoid

Never drift toward:
- photorealism
- 3D rendering
- CGI
- glossy digital painting
- smooth AI-generated gradients
- flat vector illustration
- sterile geometric illustration
- plastic textures
- hyper-sharp commercial rendering
- anime
- exaggerated cartoon styling
- generic mobile-game art
- generic SaaS illustration

---

## 3. Color Language

Color should be inspired by the supplied visual references, especially the relationship between blue sky, fresh greens, and warm cream tones.

**Use the palette family as inspiration, not as a fixed color recipe and never copy a reference composition.**

### Sky

Prefer:
- clear pastoral blue
- medium to deep blue
- clean but slightly softened tones
- natural rather than neon color

The exact sky color may vary with:
- time of day
- season
- weather
- scene
- narrative purpose

Avoid:
- electric blue
- neon cyan
- artificial gradient blue

### Landscape

Prefer a natural family of:
- fresh green
- warm yellow-green
- meadow green
- deeper forest green

Use variation rather than one flat green.

### Light neutrals

Warm cream, ivory, pale yellow, and similarly soft neutrals can be used to create warmth in:
- clouds
- paper
- light
- buildings
- highlights
- atmospheric elements

These are part of the palette language, not mandatory objects.

### Overall palette

The world should generally feel:
- sunlit
- fresh
- warm
- natural
- optimistic

Do not use neon or overly synthetic game-like palettes unless a future product decision explicitly calls for a special scene.

---

## 4. Light & Atmosphere

Default lighting:

**Soft warm daylight**

The visual reference is a peaceful countryside morning or early afternoon.

Use:
- gentle sunlight
- warm highlights
- soft shadows
- subtle atmospheric depth
- clean, calm air
- warm illumination

The exact lighting can change when the story requires it. Night, rain, sunset, winter, indoor scenes, or other environments are allowed.

What remains consistent is the **soft, tactile, illustrated treatment of light**, rather than a fixed time of day.

Avoid:
- HDR
- harsh contrast
- heavy lens effects
- artificial glow
- overly cinematic lighting that breaks the storybook world

The default emotional temperature is:

**bright · peaceful · hopeful · alive**

---

## 5. Cloud & Sky Language

Clouds are an example of the desired illustration treatment, not a required recurring element.

When clouds appear, they can be:
- soft
- expressive
- layered
- hand-drawn
- slightly oversized
- warm cream or ivory
- subtly shadowed with cool tones

A mild sense of expressive movement is welcome.

The visual reference may suggest the expressive movement of traditional painting, but **do not directly imitate any specific artist or artwork**.

The goal is:

**dreamy, hand-drawn movement — not dramatic painterly imitation.**

---

## 6. Natural Surfaces

Natural surfaces should feel alive and tactile.

When relevant, use:
- layered pencil strokes
- organic variation
- directional marks
- subtle texture
- natural imperfections
- gentle depth
- believable material differences

This applies not only to grass, but also to:
- soil
- leaves
- bark
- stone
- water
- flowers
- fur
- wood
- fields
- mountains
- natural landscapes

### Principle

**Alive, not busy.**

Texture should enrich the world without turning every surface into visual noise.

Do not require a specific grass treatment or landscape structure in every image.

---

## 7. Subject & Element Freedom

FARM FROM HERE is intentionally open-ended.

Future illustrations may feature:
- real farms
- trees
- plants
- adopted trees
- flowers
- animals
- pets
- companion creatures
- farm families
- farmers
- people
- buildings
- landscapes
- objects
- seasonal environments
- imaginative elements
- map worlds
- game-like discoveries

There is **no fixed list of required visual elements**.

Every subject should be interpreted through the same illustration language.

### Important rule

**New subject ≠ new art style.**

A newly introduced plant, animal, creature, farm, or environment should look like it belongs to the same illustrated world without being forced to resemble any previously used object.

---

## 8. Character & Creature Treatment

Characters, animals, pets, and future companion creatures should all share the same underlying illustration treatment.

They should generally have:
- hand-drawn contours
- organic shapes
- tactile colored-pencil / crayon rendering
- soft natural shading
- expressive but restrained faces
- believable anatomy or intentionally stylized anatomy appropriate to the world

Do not force every creature into the same exact character design.

The goal is:

**visual family resemblance through medium and treatment, not identical character construction.**

This allows FARM FROM HERE to introduce new companion creatures, adopted animals, pets, plants, and other discoveries without restricting creative possibilities.

---

## 9. Objects & Architecture

Objects should feel integrated into the illustrated environment.

Whether the scene contains:
- a farmhouse
- a barn
- a treehouse
- a fence
- a doorway
- a vehicle
- a bench
- a tool
- a map marker
- a whimsical game object

the object should receive the same:
- pencil/crayon treatment
- organic line quality
- warm natural lighting
- tactile surface
- restrained storybook stylization

No individual object is part of the permanent visual identity.

---

## 10. Composition Principles

The composition should generally have:

**one clear visual subject + breathing room + a natural visual flow**

Avoid filling every part of the image with objects.

Good visual hierarchy can be created through:
- scale
- placement
- contrast
- light
- negative space
- directional lines
- framing
- depth
- color relationships

The focal object does not need to be physically huge.

Create importance through composition.

### Creative freedom

Composition may vary substantially between screens.

A splash screen, login scene, farm discovery, tree detail, animal encounter, map, update card, and companion scene do not need to share the same layout.

They only need to share the same **visual language**.

---

## 11. Splash Screen Direction

The splash screen should communicate:

**This is the beginning of a journey into a real living world.**

The previously explored countryside threshold concept is a useful example, but it is **not a permanent required composition**.

Future splash concepts may use entirely different visual metaphors for:
- beginning
- discovery
- connection
- growth
- entering the real world

What must remain consistent is:
- the hand-drawn storybook medium
- warm natural palette
- tactile pencil/crayon texture
- gentle atmosphere
- sense of wonder grounded in reality

---

## 12. Login & Supporting Scenes

Supporting images should use the exact same visual language as the splash screen.

They can freely change:
- location
- composition
- weather
- season
- activity
- subject
- farm
- animal
- plant
- creature
- landscape

But they must preserve:
- pencil/crayon texture
- warm natural color relationships
- pastoral blue / natural green family where appropriate
- soft illustrated lighting
- organic linework
- storybook atmosphere

The goal is that users immediately recognize:

> **“This is the same world.”**

---

## 13. Cross-Image Consistency Rules

When generating a new FARM FROM HERE image:

1. Preserve the same illustration medium.
2. Preserve the same general visual warmth.
3. Preserve the same pencil/crayon material quality.
4. Preserve the same tactile, hand-made feeling.
5. Preserve the same general level of visual refinement.
6. Preserve the same organic line quality.
7. Preserve the same relationship between realism and whimsy.
8. Allow the subject matter to change freely.
9. Allow composition to change freely.
10. Allow new visual motifs and characters to emerge naturally.
11. Do not force previously used elements into a new scene.
12. Never introduce a new unrelated visual style merely because the subject is different.

### Critical rule

**New image ≠ new art direction.**

Every new image should look like it could have been drawn by the **same illustrator, for the same book, on the same paper, using the same materials**, even when the subject is completely different.

---

## 14. Prompt Anchor

When generating FARM FROM HERE imagery, use this visual anchor as the baseline:

> **Warm hand-drawn pastoral storybook illustration, colored pencil and soft crayon texture, visible organic pencil strokes, imperfect handmade linework, tactile paper-like texture, warm cream highlights, fresh natural greens, clear pastoral blue sky when appropriate, soft expressive clouds when present, gentle warm natural light, organic forms, peaceful optimistic atmosphere, whimsical but grounded, sophisticated and restrained, tactile and alive, nostalgic countryside picture-book feeling.**

### Negative direction

> **No photorealism, no 3D, no CGI, no glossy rendering, no vector art, no flat digital illustration, no anime, no cartoon exaggeration, no plastic texture, no hyperrealistic rendering, no neon colors, no sterile SaaS aesthetic, no generic mobile-game art.**

---

## 15. Brand-Level Definition

The shortest definition of the FARM FROM HERE visual identity is:

> **A living real world, drawn like a warm countryside storybook.**

This is the **art-direction north star**.

It defines **how things are drawn**, not **what things must appear**.

The world should remain open enough for FARM FROM HERE to discover new plants, trees, animals, pets, companion creatures, people, farms, places, and imaginative elements without breaking visual consistency.
